01 · What I found

The look was a problem. The bigger one was a product that put AI before people.

The team had built the old site with AI before I joined. Its first line was “Your agent is already dating on your behalf”. Barely any women signed up, and the early events ran nine men to every woman.

The visuals were part of it: people in research called the site a heavy magic book. The product itself, and the way it reached people, put the AI in front of the person. That is what women were turning down.

Two sources said the same thing.

33% vs 17%
Women expecting AI to affect them negatively over twenty years, against a positive effect. Among men it is close to even.Pew Research Center, 5,119 US adults, February 2026

People want to keep the choice, and a way to stay in control of the AI. That is what I designed for, in the product and on the site.

02 · What I changed

I changed how the product reaches people first, then how it looks.

01 · Reach

Keep the mystery and the privacy. A Secret Santa hint in place of a profile handover.

02 · Look

Real people, warm colour and university names in place of the heavy magic book.

03 · Clue first

She gets one hint, and she decides what happens next.

01 · How the product reaches people

The old mechanic matched two people and then swapped their photographs and Instagram handles on the spot. I took the shape from Secret Santa instead. You know your match is in the room, you get one small hint, and you decide when to open it. Nothing of hers goes across until she says yes, and she can stop at any point.

02 · How it looks

Near-black, wine and gold read as mystical. I moved the site to warm photographs of real people, a palette of blush, sage and plum, and the names of the London universities people already know.

03 · Clue first

People kept asking what Cupid actually does. So the site shows the real message she would get: one hint, and under it the line “You decide what you gonna do, always”.

04 · Why we went back

Better matches needed us to know people better.

People signed up and came along, but the matches were weak and no couples came out of them. A handful of WhatsApp questions holds almost nothing of a person. The CEO and I agreed to build understanding first, then match.

So round two became the report. You upload a conversation you have already had, with a crush, an ex or a friend, and say what you want to know. Soul reads all of it and writes back a report on the relationship. The person sees how the AI understood them, and we get to know them properly.

05 · Three directions

I tested three visual directions, and people picked the letter.

The report asks people for something far more personal, so the site had to change with it. I built the same page three ways and ran them as an A/B/C test.

Scroll inside each frame to compare the whole page.

The letter won. It points at the last time people got to know each other slowly, by writing, and it lets something personal arrive as something intimate and worth trusting.

06 · Keeping track

I watch the site with Clarity and PostHog, and change whatever the numbers point at.

  1. Track

    Clarity and PostHog run on every page.

  2. Spot

    Where people click, stall, or stop scrolling.

  3. Change

    The one part the numbers point at.

  4. Check

    Watch the same number again.

We moved from Clarity to PostHog, and we keep adding to what it tracks.

One principle ran through all of it: make people feel safe with AI

The AI works in the background and the person stays in the foreground. She can see what it is doing, and she can stop it.

Over 5,000 people use 1Soul now. What we are building towards is a product people pass to their friends.

How I work

I talk to engineering and the CEO more than anyone. I start from the AI-drawn version and put the human part back in, prototype it, and test it with people inside and outside the team. Once it reads, I hand engineering a Figma file and walk them through it on a call.

Claude for the thinking, Figma for the design, Claude Code for the build, GitHub for the ship. I put that route together myself. When I spot something small I fix it in Claude Code and merge to main, which publishes the site, so I stay with the design all the way to the live page.

AI sped up everything after each decision, and the decisions stayed with people.
