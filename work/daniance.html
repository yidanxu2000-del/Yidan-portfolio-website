2026 · Concept · AI · Fintech · 5 days

Daniance

Fintech earned people’s trust by letting them see what happened, see why, follow it backwards, and stop it. I wanted to see if an AI that answers money questions could earn trust the same way.

A finance dashboard for small business owners, where an AI answers “Can I afford to hire someone?” on the owner’s own forecast.

The question
Can a small business owner afford to hire someone? The money decision that keeps them up at night.
What I made
A web app in Figma: Home, Reports, Goals, a bank connection, and one AI that changes size by page.
Time
Five days, as a concept. Research from published surveys and accountants’ forums.

On my homepage I wrote that trust in AI gets earned the way fintech earned it. Daniance is where I tried that idea on money itself. The full case study lives in Figma ↗.

Maya needs more than a balance to know if she can hire

Maya runs a four-person design studio. Her bank says £24,300. Payroll, rent and a VAT bill go out before two clients pay, and she wants to hire a junior designer in January.

Halfof small business owners struggle to read their own numbers. Xero, 2024.
56%make decisions on incomplete information every month. Digits, 2021.

I drew the summary three times before adding any AI

A health score raised the question “72 out of what?”. Balance minus bills covered today only. The version I kept finds the lowest point in the next 30 days and keeps a £5,000 buffer, so “safe to spend” has already counted payroll and VAT.

The AI lives in a narrow card that opens across its row

I tried the assistant in three places. A chat panel was clear but took a third of the screen. An answer inside Home pushed the forecast below the fold. I kept a narrow card that opens across its row when asked.

Then each page got its own size. Owners come to Home for the whole picture, so there the AI opens up and explains. In Reports it stays a card beside the detail, and in Goals it only suggests.

The AI follows my four rules for trust

The same four I use for anything that acts on someone’s behalf.

01 · What it did

The card lists what it is checking while it works.

02 · Why

The answer sits on her own forecast, with its sources.

03 · Traceable

Any answer can become a plan her accountant reviews.

04 · Easy to stop

Bank access is read only, and she can stop it any time.

1. Showing what it did

Maya taps a suggested question. The card opens to the right across the row, the cash forecast moves down to make room, and the card lists what it’s checking while it draws the answer. Click anywhere outside and it folds back into the small card, and the forecast returns to its place.

2. Showing why

The answer sits on her own forecast: a one-line verdict, the risk (on 7 February she’d dip £460 under her buffer), three numbers, and the sources underneath. If a bank hasn’t synced, the answer is marked as a draft.

3. Making each answer traceable

Saving the answer turns it into a hiring plan that Tom, her accountant, can review. His note on the Employment Allowance brings the cost down to about £2,390 a month, and the plan shows where that came from.

4. Making access easy to stop

Bringing in another bank is modelled on the UK Open Banking customer experience guidelines: consent in Daniance, then Face ID and the choice of accounts inside the bank’s own app. Access is read only for 90 days, she can stop it any time, and if she cancels, nothing is shared.

Reports keep the table her accountant already reads

Tom reads Reports too, so the page keeps the profit and loss table he already knows. Accountants read a change as favourable or adverse whatever its sign, so I coloured each change by what it means for the business: costs going down show green. Each KPI card carries a small trend for the last four quarters.

The final design

Next: real owners

I’d run one task per flow with UK small business owners and measure two things: the time from opening Home to a confident answer, and how much they trust the reply on a 1 to 5 scale.
